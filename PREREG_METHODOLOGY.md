# How pre-registration is done in this repository

A note on process, for anyone reading the commit history. This is not part of
the manuscript.

## The problem being solved

Analyses of a fixed dataset are exposed to hindsight bias: once a result is
known, it is difficult to say honestly what one would have predicted, and easy
to construct a rationale for whatever was found. The standard remedy is to
write predictions down before looking.

The remedy has a weakness in practice. A researcher who has already seen
preliminary results, or who built the pipeline and watched it run, cannot
unsee them. "I wrote this before I looked" is unverifiable in principle.

## What is done here

Predictions are generated by a **structurally blinded agent** given a brief
that specifies exactly what it may read. The brief for the composition-gap
predictions is `scripts/blind_prereg_brief.md`, and it states:

> You have NOT seen any experimental results. Your predictions come from
> analyzing the Boolean rules (topology, feedback loops, AND-gate depth,
> pathway structure) and NOTHING ELSE.

The point is not that an agent predicts better than a person. It usually does
not. **The point is that the blinding is enforceable and auditable.** What the
predictor was permitted to read is written down, committed, and checkable. The
same guarantee cannot be given for a human co-author who has been living with
the pipeline for a week.

The brief is committed **before** the predictions exist, so the constraint is
frozen rather than described after the fact. For the composition-gap
predictions the brief was in the repository's first commit (`9e070ab`,
2026-07-28 08:13); the predictions were written that evening.

## Rules followed

1. **One pre-registration per arm, one commit each.** Bundling arms lets a
   later arm's design drift toward an earlier arm's result.
2. **The commit lands before the script exists**, and the commit message says
   so explicitly. A pre-registration written after the analysis code is not a
   pre-registration.
3. **Predictions must be able to lose.** Each names a falsifier and a decision
   rule fixed in advance, including what outcome would withdraw the claim
   entirely.
4. **Amendments are appended, never edited in.** A revised prediction is added
   as a dated amendment stating what changed and why, and whether anything had
   been run at the time. Two such amendments exist and both predate execution.
5. **Results are committed against the SHA of the pre-registration they
   answer**, so the pairing is in the history rather than in someone's memory.
6. **Failed predictions are kept and reported.** Prediction 1 predicted a
   positive correlation and measured Spearman rho = -0.509 -- wrong, and in the
   opposite direction. It is reported as a failure.

## The limitation this does not fix, stated plainly

**Blinding guarantees the predictor did not see the results. It guarantees
nothing about whether the predictor was any good.**

A null result reported as "pre-registered predictions performed at chance"
carries an ambiguity that blinding cannot resolve: the quantity may be
genuinely unpredictable from the stated features, or the predictor may simply
have been poor. Those are different findings and the prediction exercise alone
cannot separate them.

The correct remedy is not a better predictor but a different test: **fit the
structural features to the outcome directly and report the fit.** If the
features carry no information in a direct regression, the null holds regardless
of anyone's predictions, and the blinded exercise becomes corroboration rather
than evidence. Where this repository reports a prediction-based null, that
direct test is the thing a reader should ask for, and its absence is a real
limitation rather than a presentational one.

## Disclosure

Agent involvement in generating predictions is disclosed wherever a venue's
AI-use policy requires it. It is a provenance fact about how a prediction was
authored, not a result: the predictions rest on named structural features
(feedback-loop counts, rule complexity, in-degree distribution) that are
standard measures in this literature, and they stand or fall on those features
rather than on their authorship.
