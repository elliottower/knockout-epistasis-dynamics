# Pre-registration: multi-estimator recovery of order-3+ coefficients under a sample budget

**Frozen before any estimator other than LASSO is run on the 28 coalition tables.**

## Why this arm exists

The novelty audit reduced this project's defensible contribution to one sentence:

> No multi-estimator head-to-head on order >= 3 coefficient recovery, with
> error-against-budget curves scored on exact truth, exists in any of the six
> literatures searched.

That contribution has no pre-registration. `prereg_faure_generalisation_v1.md`
tests whether *one* estimator (LASSO on the Walsh basis) generalises across 28
systems. It does not run the head-to-head. This document does.

The distinction matters for the audit verdicts. Faure-generalisation answers a
replication question and is scored against a published number (r = 0.99). The
head-to-head answers the question P1/P3/P4 were narrowed to, and is the arm the
paper's remaining novelty claim rests on.

## Estimator panel (pre-registered, no additions after freeze)

The audit made three of these mandatory by name. Omitting the published
benchmarks' own methods is the fastest route to rejection.

| # | Estimator | Family | Status | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | LASSO on Walsh basis | L1 spectral | Reference | Faure et al. 2024 protocol; links this arm to the generalisation arm |
| 2 | ARDR / Bayesian compressive sensing | Bayesian sparse | **Mandatory** | Nelson et al. PRB 88 155105 (2013): beats OLS/RFE specifically in the underdetermined low-budget regime, which is our regime |
| 3 | l0l2 best subset + ridge | Combinatorial sparse | **Mandatory** | Zhong et al. PRB 106 024203 (2022); strongest published cluster-expansion recovery method |
| 4 | shapiq approximator (KernelSHAP-IQ) | Shapley-interaction | **Mandatory** | Muschalik et al. NeurIPS 2024 D&B is the closest prior benchmark; omitting its own method is indefensible |
| 5 | Additive baseline | Null model | **Mandatory** | Budget-independent adversarial floor; required post-MULTI-evolve |

Explicitly **excluded and why**, so the exclusion is on record rather than
discovered by a reviewer:

- **split-Bregman l1** (Nelson et al. PRB 87 035125): same L1 objective as slot 1,
  different solver. Redundant at the estimand level.
- **RFE-OLS, adaptive LASSO**: greedy variants of slot 1; folded into slot 1's
  implementation-variant check instead of consuming a panel slot.
- **q-SFT / GFast / ProxySPEX / SPEX**: query-model estimators that assume
  adaptive access to the oracle. Our protocol fixes a uniform random subsample in
  advance, so adaptive-query methods are not comparable on the same budget axis.
  **This is a scope declaration, not a claim they would lose.** A follow-up arm
  with an adaptive-query budget is the honest place to run them.

## Protocol

Identical subsampling to `prereg_faure_generalisation_v1.md`, deliberately, so
the two arms share an axis:

1. For each of the 28 networks and each budget `b in {1, 5, 10, 25, 50}%`, draw
   `floor(b * 2^n)` coalitions uniformly at random without replacement.
2. The empty coalition and the full coalition are always included.
3. Every estimator sees the identical subsample (paired design; the subsample is
   drawn once per (network, budget, repeat) and shared).
4. 50 repeats per (network, budget).

Pairing is not optional. Estimators must be compared on the same draws or the
between-estimator variance is contaminated by subsample variance.

## Scoring

Structure recovery is primary. Prediction accuracy is secondary. A smooth
interpolator can achieve low held-out error while recovering no correct
coefficients, and the paper's claim is about coefficients.

**Primary (order-3+ structure recovery):**

- **Order-3+ support AUROC.** Restrict to coefficients of order >= 3. Define true
  support as `|w_T| > eps`. AUROC of `|w_hat_T|` as a classifier of that support.
  This is the headline number; it is the quantity the audit says nobody has
  reported.
- **Order-3+ top-k overlap.** Jaccard between the k largest-magnitude predicted
  and true order-3+ coefficients, `k in {10, 50, 100}`.
- **Order-3+ energy error.** `|predicted E_3+ - true E_3+|` in percentage points.

**Secondary:**

- Held-out FVU on predicted `v(S)`.
- All-order coefficient Pearson r (this is Faure's metric; reported for
  comparability with the generalisation arm, *not* as the primary claim).

**Threshold definition, pinned now to avoid post-hoc drift:** `eps` is the 95th
percentile of `|w_T|` over a null distribution generated by 1,000 random sign
flips of the exact coalition table, seed 20260802, computed once per network and
stored in `results/eps_thresholds.json` before any estimator runs. The threshold
is a property of the network, not of any estimator, and is never recomputed.

**shapiq exemption.** KernelSHAP-IQ with interaction order capped at k
structurally zeroes coefficients above order k. It is scored on order-3+ metrics
**only when run at k >= 3**, and its k = 2 configuration is reported solely as a
quantification of what the standard second-order approximation costs. Scoring a
k = 2 model on order-3+ recovery measures its definition, not its performance.

## Predictions

**H1 (primary).** At `b = 1%`, no estimator exceeds 0.80 order-3+ support AUROC
median across the 28 systems. Faure's r = 0.99 is an all-order correlation
dominated by low-order coefficients; the order-3+ support problem is strictly
harder and we predict the published number does not transfer to it.

**H2 (primary).** The estimator ranking is **not constant across budget**. We
predict ARDR (slot 2) leads at `b <= 5%` and l0l2 (slot 3) leads at `b >= 25%`,
from the cluster-expansion literature's own finding that Bayesian sparse methods
dominate specifically when underdetermined. A constant ranking would mean the
budget axis carries no information and the head-to-head reduces to a single
number per estimator.

**H3 (primary).** All four non-null estimators beat the additive baseline on
order-3+ support AUROC at `b >= 10%`. If any fails to, that estimator's
complexity is unjustified at that budget and we say so.

**H4 (secondary), the cross-arm link.** Order-3+ recovery degrades with the
composition gap: rank correlation between `Delta_3+` and best-estimator AUROC is
negative, `rho <= -0.3`. This is the same hypothesis as H3 in the Faure arm,
tested with a panel rather than one method. If it holds under both, the link
between the two projects is estimator-independent and is a real finding rather
than a LASSO artifact.

**H5, the null outcome that kills the arm.** If all estimators land within 0.05
AUROC of each other at every budget, there is no head-to-head to report. The arm
collapses to a single recovery curve, merges into the Faure generalisation arm,
and the paper's novelty claim narrows again -- to "order-3+ recovery is hard,
uniformly" -- which is publishable but is a different and smaller paper.

## Decision rule, fixed in advance

| outcome | consequence |
|---|---|
| H1, H2, H3 hold | The head-to-head is the paper's central figure: AUROC-vs-budget curves, five estimators, 28 systems. This is the surviving novelty claim, delivered. |
| H1 fails (someone hits 0.80+ at 1%) | Order-3+ recovery is easier than predicted; the interesting result becomes *which* estimator and *why*, and the paper reframes toward method recommendation rather than difficulty. |
| H2 fails (constant ranking) | Report the single winner honestly. The budget axis becomes a robustness check rather than a finding. |
| **H5 fires** | Merge into the Faure arm. Do not publish a head-to-head that found no differences and dress it as one. |
| H4 holds in both arms | Cross-arm mechanism claim is licensed: systems where dynamics create the most higher-order structure are the ones where sparse recovery fails. This becomes the Discussion's strongest paragraph. |

## Implementation-variant check (MechVal E1)

Each estimator runs two defensible variants. Disagreement flags fragility and is
reported, not hidden. Method-conditional results are a known top-tier failure
mode (the IOI circuit's 87% faithfulness dropping below 50% under a different
ablation operator is the canonical case).

| Estimator | Variant A | Variant B |
|---|---|---|
| LASSO | 5-fold CV lambda | BIC-selected lambda |
| ARDR | default alpha/lambda priors | broadened priors (10x) |
| l0l2 | ridge weight from CV | fixed ridge weight 1e-3 |
| KernelSHAP-IQ | k = 3 | k = 4 |
| Additive | (none; no meaningful variant by design -- stated, not faked) |

The additive baseline has no meaningful implementation variance. Manufacturing
one (mean-clamped vs zero-clamped is the same n+1-value model in two encodings)
would be a non-test dressed as a test.

## Statistical plan

- **Family definition, stated explicitly:** 4 comparisons (each non-null
  estimator vs additive baseline) x 5 budgets = 20 tests. Holm-Bonferroni is
  applied **within each budget level independently** (4 tests per family), not
  pooled across budgets, because the budget levels answer separate questions.
- Estimator-vs-estimator comparisons are **descriptive only**. We report the
  ranking and its bootstrap CI; we do not run 10 pairwise tests per budget.
- All results reported regardless of significance. No selective reporting.

## Engineering constraint

At `n = 18`, `b = 50%`, the dense design matrix (131k x 262k float64) is ~250 GB.
All L1/L0 solvers must use implicit matrix-vector products via the fast
Walsh-Hadamard transform (butterfly operations) with coordinate descent or FISTA.
This is standard compressed-sensing-on-Hadamard practice, not a methods change.
ARDR and l0l2 must be verified to support operator-based design matrices before
the freeze; if either cannot, it is dropped **now**, with the drop recorded here,
rather than silently after seeing results.

## Not varied

The 28 networks, their coalition tables, the Walsh basis, the subsampling
protocol, the eps thresholds.

## A1.1 Budget grid inherits a degeneracy from the Faure arm

At $b = 1\%$, a system with $n = 7$ receives **one coalition** and $n = 10$
receives ten. Pooling those into a median AUROC across 28 systems measures
sample starvation, not estimator quality.

Adopting the corrections in `prereg_faure_generalisation_v1.md` Amendment 1.2 in
full: report absolute sample counts alongside relative budgets; read the 1%
condition only over $n \geq 13$; and add an absolute-budget axis of
$\{64, 256, 1024, 4096\}$ coalitions.

**H1 is therefore read over $n \geq 13$ only**, and the restriction is stated on
the figure rather than in a caption.

## A1.2 H4 is the triangulation partner of H3 in the Faure arm

Stated in both documents so it reads as design. Same hypothesis, one estimator
against five. Concordance makes the composition-gap/recovery link
estimator-independent; discordance means L1 penalisation was doing the work and
the cross-project claim does not survive.
